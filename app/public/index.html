How water is checked

Second Tap compares the numbers on each apartment's lab report with published limits. It is a first screen to save you phone calls. It is not a lab certificate: your site engineer makes the final decision.

Limits for every use

From the National Green Tribunal's 2019 order on treated sewage.

Extra limits for concrete curing and mixing

From IS 456:2000, the Indian standard for concrete. Chlorides rust the steel inside reinforced concrete, and sulphates weaken it over time.

What the three results mean

  • Fit Every value needed for that use is reported and within its limit, and the report is recent.
  • Check first Nothing fails, but something is missing or out of date. Concrete mixing always gets this result, because IS 456 also asks for strength tests.
  • Not suitable At least one value is over its limit.

What we assume

  • Distance is a straight line. The road distance will be longer.
  • A lab report older than 90 days counts as out of date. That is our own rule, not a regulation.
  • Transport cost uses the tanker size and trip cost you enter. Pipeline listings are costed without tanker trips; laying the pipe is not included.
  • Listings marked "Sample" are made-up examples for demonstration.

How the lab report is used

  • When an apartment uploads its report, an AI model on Amazon Bedrock reads the values so the manager does not have to type them.
  • When the listing is published, the typed numbers are compared with the report. If they disagree, the listing says so and cannot show "Fit" until a buyer has checked.
  • The AI can misread a blurred photo or an unusual layout. Buyers can always open the report itself.

The suggested order

  • When one apartment cannot cover your need, we suggest how much to take from each "Fit" apartment, starting with the lowest cost per KL. It is a sensible starting point, not a guaranteed cheapest answer.
